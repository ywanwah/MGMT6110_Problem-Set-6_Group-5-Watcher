The brief

What the two agents are asked to do every morning

Watcher · 6:07 AM Singapore

  • Lists every US company that filed earnings results with the SEC since its last run (8-K, item 2.02).
  • For Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, plus two companies picked at random from that day's filers: revenue, operating income, net income and diluted EPS, each with a quote from the release.
  • Writes down the guidance, two to four things management says, and the tone of the release.

Assessor · 6:37 AM Singapore

  • Checks each quote against the release and each figure against the company's quarterly filing (10-Q, via XBRL).
  • Builds a fundamentals table: this quarter, a year ago, the previous quarter, margins.
  • Judges what management says against those numbers and rates the watcher: S1 Correct, S2 Complete, S3 Useful.
Reader: an equity analyst at a Singapore fund, before the 8:30 AM meeting. Neither agent ever recommends buying or selling.
Two roles, one reader. The assessor is the control on the watcher; the analyst is the control on both.

Rules in config.json

  • Watchlist CIKs 1652044, 1018724, 1326801 · two radar picks · first run looks back 7 days.
  • A figure matches the 10-Q within 0.5%; EPS must match to the cent.
  • "Cannot check" is re-tried each morning for 10 days.
  • XBRL tags: Revenues / RevenueFromContract…, OperatingIncomeLoss, NetIncomeLoss, EarningsPerShareDiluted.

What the first weekend produced

  • Backfill run Sunday night: 365–366 results filings from 18–25 October; five companies saved per dashboard.
  • Scheduled runs Monday 6:07 and 6:37 AM: a Sunday window, so nothing new; the assessor re-checked anything still unconfirmed.
  • All four team dashboards run the same template with the same watchlist; only the random picks differ.

Dashboard 1 · ywanwah (mine)

Right numbers, a lost brief, and guidance cut short

Picks Waste Connections · IridiumConfirmed 19Cannot check 1Runs 6Open dashboard ↗

What held up

  • Meta, Amazon and Iridium figures and quotes match exhibit 99.1; 19 of 20 figures confirmed to the 10-Q.
  • Tone calls are sound: Meta "balanced"; Iridium's "positive" ruled not supported because operating income fell 54.9%.
  • Radar lists 365 filers with the watchlist flagged, capped at 300 rows with a note.

What went wrong

  • Brief overwritten. Briefs are keyed by date; the 11:41 PM re-run replaced the 11:27 PM brief that recorded 365 filings and 5 extractions with "No new results today". The run log still links to it.
  • Guidance incomplete. Amazon also guided Q3 operating income ($22.5–26.5bn); Meta also guided capex ($135–145bn). Assessor scored Complete 5/5.
  • A recheck that cannot succeed. Waste Connections' 10-Q is filed but no longer tags NetIncomeLoss; the page promises a confirmation "until the 10-Q is filed" that will never come.
  • Ambiguous quotes. "Revenues | $ | 96,428 | $ | 119,796" vs Meta's 2026-first order; the reader cannot tell which column is this quarter.
Own the misses first. The overwrite is the one that would embarrass an analyst: the day's most important brief is gone and the run log links to the wrong file.

Finding in detail

The 6:07 AM run reads a cache from the night before

GitHub fetch filled sec_cache/Watcher read the cacheAssessor ranNew York after-close filing window on a weekday (4:00–5:30 PM ET)

On Sunday nothing was filed, so nothing was missed. On a weekday the same timing means the watcher reads answers fetched six to nine hours before the filings it exists to catch. Tuesday's sources line is the test: it should name a fetch after about 5:30 AM Singapore, not the previous evening.

Hover the dots for exact times. This is the one finding that applies identically to all four dashboards, because the fetch design is shared.
Credit where due

What the assessor already does well

Arithmetic

67 of 67 confirmed figures equal the 10-Q value to the last digit; EPS to the cent. Thousands versus millions handled (2,638,081 → 2,638.1). Not one wrong number across four dashboards.

Independence

All four assessors marked Meta's "investments are paying off" as not matching a 36.8% fall in operating income. Two overruled the watcher's tone outright. One ruled Iridium's upbeat tone unsupported against a 54.9% profit fall.

Honesty about limits

"Cannot check" is used rather than guessed; the reasons name the tag, the period and the filing date; segment claims are labelled as untestable from the fundamentals.

Hand-off to the analyst

Briefs carry the release link, the accession number and a "check by hand" list. The page says plainly that the reader is the second check.

Balance the critique: the agents are good at exactly the thing people fear they are bad at, numbers. The weakness is knowing what is missing.

Before Alphabet and Amazon report Q3

Seven fixes, in order of what they protect

  1. Refresh the cache before 6:07 AM or force the fetch at run time; the morning run must read that evening's New York filings, not the previous night's.
  2. Name briefs by run time and never overwrite a brief that saved results; keep the run log pointing at the brief that run wrote.
  3. Guidance as a list (metric, period, low, high, quote) in PROMPT_WATCH.md; the assessor re-reads the outlook section and docks S2 for anything missed.
  4. Confirm a radar pick is an earnings release before saving it; if the exhibit has no income statement, record why and draw another company.
  5. Bank rule: revenue = net interest income + noninterest income, operating income = income before taxes, both components quoted and named.
  6. Freeze scores between rechecks unless a new filing arrives, and let S1 carry the doubt when the 10-Q is not yet filed.
  7. Quote the column header with the row, add fallback XBRL tags, and say "10-Q filed, tagged differently" instead of promising a confirmation that cannot come.
Tick items as they ship; the list remembers in this browser.
Fixes 1 and 2 are plumbing and can ship today. 3 to 5 are prompt changes. 6 and 7 are polish that stops the page over-promising.
The outcome

Every number was right. The page still needed a human.

Across four dashboards the agents did not get a single figure wrong, and the assessor caught the one claim that mattered most. What they could not see was what was missing: a second guidance line, a bank's revenue, a pick that was never an earnings release, a cache that was nine hours old. That is the gap the analyst's own check has to cover, and the seven fixes are how to close most of it before the next earnings day.
